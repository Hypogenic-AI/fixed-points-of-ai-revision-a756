\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\para{Iterative self-refinement.} A model can improve its own output by generating feedback
and revising. Self-Refine~\citep{madaan2023selfrefine} is the canonical loop, fusing critique
and revision in a single prompt; Reflexion~\citep{shinn2023reflexion} accumulates verbal
self-feedback across attempts; and CRITIC~\citep{gou2023critic} critiques then corrects using
\emph{external tools} for the signal. Our operator is a structured variant of this family,
but we deliberately \emph{decouple} critique from revision into two separate calls and
regenerate the review at every step, so the critique is a first-class object we can log,
ablate, and hold responsible for the dynamics.

\para{Recursive generation as a dynamical system.} \citet{wu2026converge} is the direct
precedent: it frames recursive \emph{single-step} self-refinement as ``textual relaxation''
toward a model-preferred soft fixed point, and defines the metrics we adopt---normalized
Levenshtein transition magnitude, exact ($V_{t+1}=V_t$) and approximate
($\dt<0.02$ and relative word-count change $<0.03$ for two consecutive steps) fixed points,
and an exponential-relaxation fit $\dt \approx A e^{-kt}+c$. On ICML abstracts they report
$100\%$ approximate convergence and near-perfect fidelity. We replicate this behavior with our
single-stage baseline and show that it \emph{breaks} once an explicit review stage is
inserted. Related framings cast self-correction as feedback control with stability
thresholds~\citep{selfcontrol2026} and report independent evidence of ChatGPT
self-convergence~\citep{xylogiannopoulos2026}, but none isolate a decoupled review loop.

\para{Limits of self-correction.} A skeptical camp warns that recursion need not help.
\citet{huang2023cannot} show LLMs often cannot self-correct reasoning without an external
signal and can \emph{degrade} results. \citet{xu2024pride} show that iterative
self-refinement can \emph{amplify} a model's own bias, inflating self-perceived quality while
true quality stalls---which is precisely why we use a judge from a different model generation
than the reviser. \citet{feedbackfriction2025} find that models resist fully incorporating
even high-quality feedback, implying residual, non-converging edit pressure. Our results
reconcile the two camps: the convergent relaxation of~\citet{wu2026converge} holds for the
single-stage operator, while the skeptics' failure modes manifest precisely once an explicit
critique loop is added.

\para{Model collapse and self-consuming loops.} \citet{shumailov2023curse} and
\citet{alemohammad2023selfconsuming} study what happens when generative models consume their
own outputs, showing loss of diversity and quality (``model collapse,'' ``model autophagy
disorder''). These results are framed for \emph{training} loops; our setting is
\emph{inference-time} document revision, but the degenerate-attractor intuition transfers: we
observe not variance collapse but its dual, unbounded expansion with semantic drift. Unlike
this line of work, we do not fine-tune; we treat recursion as repeated inference and measure
its fixed-point behavior directly.
